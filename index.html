How We Search

A visible search from brief to close.

Structured enough to stay aligned. Responsive enough to change when the evidence does.

Grey Horse runs technical searches through five connected stages: calibrate the role, map the market, engage relevant candidates, assess what the market is showing, and keep momentum through the close. The process gives the search structure without making it rigid.

The Search

Five stages. One connected search.

A strong search should become clearer as it progresses. Each stage has a specific job, but the information created in one stage informs what happens next.

01
Calibrate

Get clear on the hire.

Before defining the market, get clear on the work. Grey Horse starts by understanding what the person will need to own, what the team and company need from the hire, which requirements genuinely matter, and where the brief is still uncertain. A title can describe the search. It should not define it.

We are trying to understand
  • What does this person need to accomplish?
  • What should be true in the first 12 months?
  • Which requirements are essential?
  • Which assumptions still need testing?
What gets clearerThe mandate.
02
Map

Build the right market.

Once the mandate is clearer, the search turns outward. Grey Horse maps the companies, technical environments, adjacent talent pools and role titles most likely to contain relevant people. That market may be wider than the obvious competitors.

We are trying to understand
  • Where are people solving comparable problems?
  • Which adjacent markets should be included?
  • Are we searching too narrowly by title?
  • Which environments produce the strongest profiles?
What gets clearerThe market.
03
Engage

Create real interest.

Finding someone relevant is only part of the search. They also need a credible reason to engage. Outreach gives strong candidates enough context to understand the role, the technical problem, why the hire matters and what may make the opportunity worth exploring.

We are trying to understand
  • Are the right people engaging?
  • What is creating genuine interest?
  • Which questions or objections repeat?
  • What needs to be explained more clearly?
What gets clearerCandidate response.
04
Assess

Turn signal into judgment.

As candidates enter the process, the search becomes less theoretical. Grey Horse keeps candidate conversations, client feedback and what the market is showing connected as the shortlist develops. The objective is not simply to produce more profiles.

We are trying to understand
  • Which profiles are consistently strongest?
  • Where is the brief being validated?
  • Where is it being challenged?
  • What becomes more important with real candidates?
What gets clearerThe shortlist.

Close

Keep momentum through the finish.

The search does not stop when the right candidate is identified. Timing, motivation, expectations and decision-making can all affect the final stage. Grey Horse stays close to those signals through the offer and acceptance process so important context does not disappear when it matters most.

We are trying to understand
  • What matters most to the candidate?
  • Are expectations aligned?
  • Is anything likely to slow the decision?
  • What needs attention before the offer?

Search Visibility

The search should never become a black box.

Useful information appears long before a placement is made. Candidate response, recurring questions, market availability and client feedback all reveal whether the search is moving in the right direction.

01

Market response

Are relevant candidates engaging? Patterns in interest, hesitation and objections can show whether the market understands the opportunity and whether the search is reaching the right people.

02

Candidate context

A profile only shows part of the picture. Conversations reveal motivation, timing, career direction and the conditions under which a strong candidate may consider making a move.

03

Client feedback

Each real candidate makes the brief more tangible. Feedback helps distinguish what is genuinely important from what initially looked important on paper.

Visibility matters because better information leads to better search decisions.

Calibration

The search should get sharper as evidence comes in.

The initial brief matters, but it is still an assumption about where the right candidate is likely to be found. Once the search reaches the market, real evidence may show that part of that assumption needs to change.

01

The title is too narrow.

Strong candidates may be doing comparable work under different titles.

ResponseWiden the search without weakening the underlying requirement.
02

The target market is wrong.

The expected companies may produce weaker profiles than an adjacent technical environment.

ResponseFollow the evidence into a more relevant talent pool.
03

Expectations and market reality are misaligned.

Candidate conversations may reveal a recurring gap around scope, seniority, compensation or responsibility.

ResponseSurface and address the mismatch instead of pushing the same search harder.
04

The role itself becomes clearer.

Real profiles often make it easier to separate what the company genuinely needs from what seemed important when the search began.

ResponseRefine the mandate while protecting the outcome the hire needs to deliver.
