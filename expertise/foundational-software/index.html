Foundational Software

The engineers who build what the product depends on.

Founding Engineers / Senior ICs / Hands-on Engineering Leads

Grey Horse supports software searches where one hire can materially shape the product, architecture and engineering team that follows. These are often early or high-ownership roles where the brief is still evolving and the company needs someone who can do more than execute against a finished roadmap.

The Search

Roles with real technical ownership.

The common thread is not title. It is ownership. Grey Horse works on foundational software searches where the person joining will influence how the product is built, how technical decisions are made and how the engineering team develops around them.

01

Founding & Early Engineers

For teams moving from founder-led development into a dedicated engineering function, or adding the first few engineers around an existing product.

Typical roles
  • Founding Engineer
  • Early Software Engineer
  • Backend / Platform Engineer
  • Full-Stack Engineer
What usually matters

Can this person build with incomplete information, make sound trade-offs and take responsibility beyond a narrow job description?

02

Senior Individual Contributors

For companies that need more technical depth around core systems, architecture or product development.

Typical roles
  • Senior Software Engineer
  • Staff Engineer
  • Principal Engineer
  • Senior Backend / Platform Engineer
What usually matters

Does the role need someone to build from zero, stabilize what already exists, or take a system through its next stage of scale?

03

Hands-on Engineering Leads

For roles that combine technical ownership with growing responsibility for people, systems or engineering direction.

Executive and C-level search belongs under Technical Leadership.
Typical roles
  • Engineering Lead
  • Technical Lead
  • Early Head of Engineering
What usually matters

The role still needs to stay close to the work. Once the search becomes primarily about executive leadership, organizational scale or C-level responsibility, it moves into Grey Horse’s Technical Leadership practice.

Search Context

The job title is usually the easy part.

01 / 03
01

The role is still changing.

An early software brief can move quickly. What begins as “Senior Software Engineer” may turn out to require architecture, product judgment, infrastructure ownership or informal technical leadership.

What does this person need to own in the first 12 months?
02

The same work appears under different titles.

Strong candidates may look very different on paper. Someone doing foundational work at one company may be called a Founding Engineer, Staff Engineer, Tech Lead, Platform Engineer or Senior Software Engineer. The work matters more than matching the wording of the brief.

03

Stage changes what “good” looks like.

A strong engineer inside a mature organization is not automatically the right engineer for a company still defining its product and technical systems. Foundational hires often need to operate with broader ownership and less structure.

  • Fewer established processes
  • Broader ownership
  • Imperfect information
  • Changing priorities

The right profile depends on what the company needs next, not just what the candidate has done before.

The Market

We look for the signals behind the title.

The strongest candidate does not always come from the obvious competitor or carry the obvious job title. Grey Horse looks for evidence that someone has already operated in an environment similar to the one they are being hired into.

01

Built in ambiguity

They have shipped meaningful work before every requirement, system or process was fully defined.

02

Owned more than their remit

They have taken responsibility beyond a narrow feature area or formal title.

03

Made consequential technical decisions

Their work involved architecture, trade-offs or systems that mattered to the wider product.

04

Worked through transition

They have experienced a meaningful change in company stage, product maturity, architecture or team structure.

The search starts with the environment the person needs to succeed in, then works backward into the market.
